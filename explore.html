What · for whom · why

Orientation before participation. Traceability afterwards.

Finding public information, understanding it and acting independently are different tasks. After participation, another question arises: what happened to my contribution?

For residents and other eligible participants

Understand the purpose, deadlines and participation routes, check official sources and ask focused questions. Eligibility depends on the particular process.

Aim: decide independently and assess an institution's response.

For municipalities and public institutions

Document responsibilities, review criteria, decisions and next steps in understandable terms.

Aim: show how contributions are handled and what remains unresolved.

For educators and civil society facilitators

Libraries, education providers, youth workers and community organisations can use the example for discussion and practice.

Aim: support people in expressing their own voice.

Support without a participation barrier: nobody should have to pass a learning test to participate. Choosing not to take part can also be an informed, independent decision.

For institutions · Review and learning

What happens to a contribution?

Next Level makes the requirements for a traceable response concrete. The current prototype provides a fictional example. Real use requires responsible teams, resources and genuine scope for participation to influence a decision.

Assign responsibility

Who receives contributions? Who reviews them? Who decides, and when will a response be provided?

Explain decisions

Which criteria and records support a decision? Why is a proposal adopted, changed or declined?

Verify implementation

What was announced, decided or actually implemented? Which evidence is still missing?

Learn from barriers

Which problems recur? Do they concern language, responsibilities, resources or everyday practices? Who will own an improvement and review it later?

Development goal: turn participation experience into verifiable improvements to communication and processes. The prototype does not yet implement an institutional learning process.

What happens after participation?

A fictional record makes the return path tangible. It is not a real submission, tracking system, government response or verified outcome.

Fictional demonstration Case CRF-DEMO-001

01 · Received

Input acknowledged

A resident's proposal about safer neighbourhood crossings has been recorded in the fictional consultation. A receipt would identify the channel and next steps.

02 · Reviewed

Assessment made visible

The fictional mobility office examines feasibility, accessibility, safety, budget constraints and alternative measures. The example does not claim that a real review occurred.

03 · Decision

Proposal not adopted as submitted

In this invented case, the proposed crossing location is not approved because it conflicts with an existing junction safety plan. This is an illustrative rationale, not a real engineering finding.

04 · Public response

Reasons and next steps published

The fictional authority explains the decision and states that alternative pedestrian-safety measures will be considered in a future planning round.

05 · Visible outcome and learning

Traceable, but not automatically successful

The record distinguishes an explained decision from a demonstrated change on the ground. Actual implementation, follow-up and recurring barriers would require independent verification.

Design hypothesis: an institution can make its response legible without promising that every contribution will be adopted. Whether this improves trust must be empirically tested.

Next step · Proposed pilot

Test the additional value.

Can people use a participation process more independently and assess institutional replies better with the additional learning support than with existing process information alone?

A bounded case

A proposal that can still be influenced, a responsible public institution and a local education or community organisation. Scope, eligibility, response arrangements and handling capacity are agreed before starting.

Tasks beyond clicks

Find the right route, explain the scope for influence and deadline, develop your own position and formulate a useful question. Attempt a different case after a delay.

Examine the institution too

Who replies, and by when? Which reasons and records are provided? What workload is involved? Which barrier leads to an assigned process change and a later review?

Voluntary and open-ended: disagreement, justified distrust and informed non-participation are legitimate outcomes. The learning exercise is not a condition for participation.

Planning stage. No pilot partnership is claimed. Without genuine scope for influence and responsible handling, testing is limited to an educational simulation.
